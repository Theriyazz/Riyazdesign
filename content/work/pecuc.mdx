<ContextCard>
  PECUC has protected children's rights in Odisha since 1990: 178 projects across 30 districts, UN ECOSOC status, and UNICEF among its partners. Its website dated from 2001, so I redesigned all 15 pages.

  I didn't document this as I built it, so this is what I found and decided. The old site is still live at pecuc.org to check.
</ContextCard>

## 01 — The problem

- The old site opened with an eleven-message slider and seventeen links above the fold, before a single sentence saying what PECUC does.
- Furthermore, it made the impact invisible.
- Donation and volunteer pages were broken.

<ImageBlock src="/work/pecuc/01.avif" alt="Full-page capture of the 2001-era PECUC homepage" caption="The site as it stood: an eleven-message slider, seventeen links above the fold, and photographs reduced to thumbnails." />

## 02 — The audit

I didn't run user research here. I audited the live site, and counting was enough to set the direction.

<Highlight lead="The content was never the problem." strong="It was buried behind links labelled id=H1." />

<Findings>
  <Finding n="37" title="Navigation destinations">
    Nine main items, eight utility links and twenty more in dropdowns. Everything equally important, so nothing was.
  </Finding>
  <Finding n="09" title="Disconnected properties">
    A blog, galleries, press, reports and raw Word files, each a different visual language and a dead end.
  </Finding>
  <Finding n="03" title="Accounts of what year it was">
    The footer said 2023, the news said 2025, the homepage said thirty-six years. All on one page.
  </Finding>
</Findings>

## 03 — Restructuring

<Decision
  decision="Cut 37 destinations to 7, and 9 sub-sites to one hub"
  why="A visitor comes to understand the work, verify the organisation, give money or offer time. The menu follows them, not the org chart."
  tradeoff="Compliance pages moved one click deeper, and everything depends on one publishing model."
/>

## 06 — Designing the two broken journeys

There was no funnel to improve. Donation and volunteering both had to be designed from a blank page.

<Decision
  decision="Five named tiers, not an open amount"
  why="An open field is one field but an infinite decision (Hick's law). Named tiers tied to real programmes make it a fast pick, with FCRA and ISO trust signals at the moment of commitment."
  tradeoff="I set the amounts and programme mappings without talking to a past donor."
/>

<Decision
  decision="Replace the volunteer PDF with a staged signup"
  why="The old flow had six steps: download, print, fill by hand, find an address, email, wait."
  tradeoff="Multi-step forms can raise drop-off, so the easiest questions come first."
/>

<Steps>
  <Step n="01" name="Who you are">Name, contact, location — everything a volunteer can answer without thinking.</Step>
  <Step n="02" name="What you're drawn to">Which programmes and causes they want to work on.</Step>
  <Step n="03" name="Availability">When and how often, so the match is real before anyone follows up.</Step>
</Steps>

### What I learned

<Takeaways>
  <Takeaway title="Document as I go">
    I reconstructed this reasoning afterwards. **The reasoning held; the record didn't.**
  </Takeaway>
  <Takeaway title="Test the tiers with a real donor">
    I set the amounts without talking to anyone who had given to PECUC before.
  </Takeaway>
  <Takeaway title="Design for the content editor">
    The cards work when filled correctly, but I didn't solve for a headline twice as long as the template expects.
  </Takeaway>
  <Takeaway title="Visual design is an argument, not a coat of paint">
    Every choice here makes one claim: this organisation and its work are real.
  </Takeaway>
</Takeaways>
